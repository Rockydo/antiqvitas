# M4 location-name correction sources

These codes support only the bounded display-label claims recorded in
`location_name_corrections.csv`. They do not establish exact civic boundaries,
population, language uniformity, or political ownership.

- `RB-SPRING`: Roman Baths, [Roman Sacred Spring](https://www.romanbaths.co.uk/roman-sacred-spring-0), records an earthen bank showing that the spring was a focus of worship before the Roman temple and baths. It supports the conservative `Sulis` field proxy, not a documented pre-Roman town.
- `RB-TEMPLE`: Roman Baths, [The Roman Temple](https://www.romanbaths.co.uk/roman-temple-0), describes the monumental temple complex as a Roman development. It prevents projecting the complete `Aquae Sulis` urban/sanctuary label into AD 1.
- `HE-CHESTER`: Historic England, [Bonewaldesthorne Tower on the City Walls, Chester](https://historicengland.org.uk/education/schools-resources/educational-images/bonewaldesthorne-tower-on-the-city-walls-chester-cheshire-ioe01-09358-02), dates the legionary fortress of Deva to AD 78-86. It supports removing the later `Victrix` fortress title; the shorter `Deva` remains a contested inherited-name proxy.
- `LEIZA-COL`: Leibniz Centre for Archaeology, [Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium-Cologne](https://www1.leiza.de/transformation/deutschland/staedteniedergemanienbilder/koeln/koeln_engl.htm), distinguishes the early `oppidum Ubiorum` from the colonia founded around AD 50. It supports `Oppidum Ubiorum` at the campaign start.
- `KVB-COL`: Kölner Verkehrs-Betriebe, [Köln im 1.-3. Jahrhundert](https://www.kvb.koeln/unternehmen/projekte/nord-sued-stadtbahn/archaeologie/stadtentwicklung/koeln_1-3_Jh.html), places the Ubian oppidum before the Claudian colonia. It is a corroborating urban-archaeology source for the same bounded correction.
- `HE-LINCOLN`: Historic England, [319 High Street, Lincoln](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1388590), records a first-century legionary fortress later transformed into the civilian settlement `Colonia Lindum`. It supports removing the later colonia title; `Lindum` is retained as a contested inherited-name proxy.
- `MOLA-LON`: Museum of London Archaeology, [Extensive Roman remains found in West London](https://www.mola.org.uk/discoveries/news/extensive-roman-remains-found-west-london), states that Londinium was founded about AD 48 on an uninhabited site. It rules out `Londinium` in AD 1.
- `CAES-BG-5.11`: Julius Caesar, *De bello Gallico* 5.11, [Perseus/Scaife text record](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:latinLit:phi0448.phi001.perseus-lat2:5.11.1/), attests the Thames as `Tamesis` before the campaign start. It supports a river-field proxy, not a pre-Roman London settlement.

- `HE-DORCHESTER`: Historic England, [Poundbury Camp, associated monuments and section of Roman aqueduct](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1013337), places the Iron Age hillfort in the Durotrigian tribal area and states that Durnovaria was founded alongside it soon after the invasion. It supports a Durotriges regional proxy, not an attested settlement name at the installed field.
- `UOE-EXETER`: University of Exeter, [Exeter: A Place in Time — the project](https://www.exeter.ac.uk/research/projects/archaeology/place_in_time/theproject/), dates Exeter's beginning as a legionary fortress to about AD 55 and its later transformation into a civitas capital. It rules out the complete Roman urban label in AD 1.
- `OCD-ISCA`: Oxford Classical Dictionary, [Isca](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/549652713), identifies `Isca` as a British river-name and dates the Exeter fortress to about AD 55. It supports retaining only the inherited hydronym as a contested field proxy.
- `GCC-GLOUCESTER`: Gloucester City Council, [Archaeology & Monuments](https://www.gloucester.gov.uk/planning-development/conservation-regeneration/archaeology-monuments/), dates Gloucester's Roman fortress to the AD 60s and the colonia to the late first or early second century. It rules out Glevum as an AD 1 city label.
- `CAM-DOBUNNI`: Della Hooke and Maren Clegg Hyer, introduction to *Water and the Environment in the Anglo-Saxon World* (Liverpool University Press), [Cambridge Core record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/water-and-the-environment-in-the-anglosaxon-world/introduction/4BB7ADEDAFA43E30A598DCE631A313E6), describes the Severn basin through Gloucestershire and Worcestershire as the core of the late Iron Age Dobunni. It supports the deliberately regional `Dobunni` proxy.
- `YAT-YORK`: York Archaeology, [History of Fishergate](https://fishergate.yorkarchaeology.co.uk/fishergate-york/history-of-fishergate/), dates the foundation of Eboracum to around AD 71. It rules out the Roman fortress-city at the campaign start.
- `EH-BRIGANTES`: English Heritage, [Cartimandua, Queen of the Brigantes](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/learn/histories/women-in-history/cartimandua/), places the Roman conquest of the Brigantes in AD 71-74 and a legion at York in that conquest setting. It supports `Brigantes` only as an explicit regional field proxy.

- `RPC-ANTIOCH`: Roman Provincial Coinage, [RPC I 4151](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coins/1/4151), dates an Antioch issue to 5 BC and records the Greek Antiochene civic ethnic. It anchors the Greek public-name path immediately before the campaign start.
- `OCD-ANTIOCH`: Oxford Classical Dictionary, [Antioch, Seleucid royal capital city](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548066265), identifies Antioch as the Roman provincial capital and explicitly notes its large Aramaic-speaking community. It supports parallel Hellenic and Semitic adapters rather than a Persian one.
- `RPC-LAODICEIA`: Roman Provincial Coinage, [RPC I 4431](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coins/1/4431), dates a Laodicea ad Mare issue to 2/1 BC and gives the Greek Laodicean civic ethnic. It directly anchors the campaign-boundary form `Laodikeia`.
- `DARE-BEROIA`: Digital Atlas of the Roman Empire, [Beroia, Aleppo](https://imperium.ahlfeldt.se/places/21814), records `Beroia` at Aleppo from about 330 BC through AD 640 and links the exact Pleiades place. It supports retaining the period civic name while correcting the language lookup.
- `RPC-DAMASCUS`: Roman Provincial Coinage, [RPC I 4786](https://rpc.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/coins/1/4786), dates a Damascene civic issue to 30/29 BC and records the Greek Damascene ethnic. It anchors `Damaskos` at the campaign boundary.
- `OUP-GREEK-SYRIA`: Maurice Sartre, [The Greek Cities in Roman Syria](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/59226/chapter-abstract/498427109), explains that Greek was the official language of Syrian poleis while traditional local languages and institutions survived. It supports paired Hellenic and Semitic localization adapters, not demographic uniformity.

- `HE-VENTA`: Historic England, [Venta Icenorum: Roman town and associated remains](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1021463), states that the civitas capital was laid out around AD 70 and lies five miles south of Norwich. It supports replacing the generated town label with an Iceni regional proxy at the campaign start.
- `EH-ISURIUM`: English Heritage, [History of Aldborough Roman Site](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/aldborough-roman-site/history/), states that Isurium began as a trading settlement around AD 70 and became the civilian capital only about AD 120. It rules out Isurium Brigantum in AD 1.
- `CADW-CAERWENT`: Cadw, [Caer-went Roman Town](https://cadw.gov.wales/visit/places-to-visit/caer-went-roman-town), dates Venta Silurum to about AD 75-80 after the Silures were conquered. It supports the tribal-area proxy and also makes clear that the archaeological town is Caerwent rather than the installed Monmouth point.

- `JORDAN-AMMAN`: Jordan Tourism Board, [History & Culture in Jordan](https://international.visitjordan.com/whattodo/history-culture/), identifies Iron Age Rabbath-Ammon's later name as Philadelphia and the city as a member of the Decapolis. It anchors the period civic name at the installed Amman field.
- `UNESCO-BOSRA`: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, [Ancient City of Bosra](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/22/), identifies Bosra as the northern capital of the Nabataean kingdom and dates its incorporation into Rome to AD 106. It rules out an Iranian adapter at the campaign start.
- `IAA-AKKO`: Israel Antiquities Authority, [Finds from the Hellenistic Favissa at Akko-Ptolemais](https://publications.iaa.org.il/atiqot/vol57/iss1/14), documents a Hellenistic assemblage at Akko-Ptolemais with western Greek material and Mediterranean imports. It supports a Hellenic civic path at a local Phoenician port.
- `OUP-PHOENICIA`: Getzel M. Cohen, [Phoenicia](https://academic.oup.com/california-scholarship-online/book/32975/chapter-abstract/278902166), treats Ptolemais/Antioch Ake among the Hellenistic settlements of Phoenicia. It supports Phoenician and Hellenic rather than Judean-only adapters.
- `UNESCO-BAALBEK`: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, [Baalbek](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/294/), identifies the site as a Phoenician city known as Heliopolis in the Hellenistic period and describes the Romanized triad as essentially Phoenician. It directly supports paired Phoenician and Hellenic adapters.
